The challenge

Marketing performance was highly visible at the top of the funnel, but lead volume alone could not answer whether acquisition was producing commercially useful prospects. A campaign could generate hundreds of inexpensive leads while creating little value for sales.

The CRM contained outcomes including Qualified Hot, Qualified, Converted, Not Responding, On Hold, Wrong Number, Not Interested, Call Back, Not Qualified and Offer Sent. The goal was to turn this operational CRM history into a performance view that connected acquisition with sales readiness.

The approach

Metric definition: This case study measures Sales Qualified Leads (SQLs), not Marketing Qualified Leads (MQLs). MQL rates may be materially higher because they represent an earlier funnel stage. The percentages shown here therefore represent the stricter sales qualification stage.

I grouped contacts by CRM creation month and compared total acquisition volume against their current lead status. Qualified and Qualified Hot were treated as the Sales Qualified Lead (SQL) outcome, while Not Qualified was tracked independently. Remaining statuses were retained as a separate group rather than incorrectly treating every non qualified contact as a failed lead.

This created a monthly cohort view that made changes in lead quality visible alongside changes in volume.

The key finding

The month generating the most leads was not the month generating the strongest lead quality.

March generated 747 leads and 121 sales qualified contacts, a 16.2% SQL rate. July generated only 553 leads but produced 139 sales qualified contacts, resulting in a 25.1% SQL rate.

July therefore generated approximately 26% fewer total leads than March while producing 18 more sales qualified leads. Qualification efficiency improved by roughly 55% on a relative basis.

Moving beyond CPL

Cost Per Lead is useful for acquisition efficiency, but it can become misleading when treated as the primary success metric. The analysis shifted the measurement framework toward downstream quality.

Ad Spend→Leads→Sales Sales Qualified Leads→Opportunities→Customers→Revenue

The more useful optimization metrics become SQL Rate, Cost Per Sales Qualified Lead, Lead to Customer Rate, Cost Per Conversion and ultimately revenue based efficiency.

External context and correlation

The reporting period also overlapped with significant regional geopolitical developments around the end of February 2026. CRM volume increased from 271 leads in February to 747 in March, an increase of approximately 176% month over month.

Important: this should not be presented as proof that geopolitical events caused the increase. Media spend, campaign launches, targeting, creative performance, market demand and lead capture changes could also explain the movement. The external event is a contextual variable for investigation, not a causal conclusion.

CRM data quality insight

The analysis also exposed a measurement dependency: historical reporting is only as reliable as CRM discipline. August 2025 contains 86 contacts but no contacts currently classified as Sales Qualified. This may reflect genuine outcomes, but it could also reflect incomplete historical status updates or changes in sales process.

CRM discipline is part of marketing measurement.

If sales outcomes are not consistently recorded, marketing loses visibility into which campaigns actually create business value.

MQL vs SQL

Marketing Qualified Leads and Sales Qualified Leads answer different questions. An MQL indicates that a lead has met marketing level criteria and appears worth progressing. An SQL represents the stricter point at which the lead has been accepted or identified as commercially relevant for sales.

Total Leads→MQL→SQL→Opportunity→Customer

For this reason, an MQL rate in the 40% to 60% range cannot be compared directly with the 13.5% SQL rate shown in this analysis. They measure different stages of the funnel.

What I would analyze next

The next stage is to join CRM outcomes with advertising spend and attribution data at channel, campaign, audience, creative and month level. This would allow performance to be evaluated through Cost Per Sales Qualified Lead and customer outcomes rather than lead volume alone.

The objective changes from “generate more leads” to “generate more commercially valuable outcomes from each unit of marketing investment.”
